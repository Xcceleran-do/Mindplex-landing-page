Beats

A beat is a topic desk with a human owner

Everything OmegaPlex does happens inside a beat. It holds the sources to watch, the methodology to follow, the output formats to produce, and later, its own memory.

  • Name and descriptionWhat the beat covers. The description feeds the search and the writer.
  • StatusDraft, active, paused or archived. Only an active beat runs.
  • OwnerThe editor whose name goes on every draft.
  • Co-author creditCredit OmegaClaw beside the owner, or leave the byline to the owner alone.
Why it matters

Each beat is a separate reporter with its own watchlist, voice and accountability. Add a beat, and you have added a desk, not a prompt.

Sources and discovery

Decide where it may look

Editors list the sites a beat watches. When someone clicks Run ingestion, the beat's methodology plans the searches, results are cleaned and de-duplicated, and every candidate keeps a record of how it was found.

  • SourcesName, address, priority, on or off. Higher priority sources go first.
  • Broader web discoveryOff: only the listed sites count. On: the beat may also search the open web, but never adds sources itself.
  • Enhanced searchA paid search with publication dates and tighter filtering. Falls back to standard search.
  • LimitsHow many queries, results per query, and candidates to keep. Defaults are sensible.
  • Freshness windowHow old a story may be. Read from the methodology, or set in days.
Why it matters

The beat cannot wander. Every candidate carries its provenance, so an editor can always answer "why is this here".

Runs start on a click. The schedule field in the form does nothing yet.

Prompts

One methodology, many formats

Two kinds of prompt live on a beat. The editorial prompt is the methodology: what counts as relevant, what counts as evidence, what to ignore. Output prompts are the formats: each one is a named brief that produces its own draft from the same curated run.

  • Editorial promptPlain language. Every save is a new version. Restore any version with one click.
  • Add output promptName it and describe the format. "Article", "X thread", "Newsletter", anything.
  • Word boundsOptional minimum and maximum length per format. The writer is held to it.
Why it matters

An editor adds a prompt called "X thread" with its own voice and a cap of twelve posts. From then on, every run produces a thread-shaped draft next to the article, ready to paste. Same research, no extra work.

Drafts stay inside Mindplex. Nothing posts to X or a newsletter on its own yet.

The Studio

The curator explains, the writer drafts, the editor decides

After a run, the curator ranks what is worth writing about and says why. The writer turns the shortlist into a draft per format, citing only pages it actually opened. The editor reviews, revises by chat, and publishes from the normal post editor.

  • AnalysisThe shortlist, each story's reason and label: new story, update, continuation, new angle, already covered, noise.
  • GenerateOne button per output prompt. Turns into "Ready for review" with a real title and excerpt.
  • Chat bar"Tighten the lede." "Add the cost figure." The draft is revised and the history is kept.
  • AI assistExcerpt, tags, categories and a featured image, one click each, priced in credits.
  • PublishIn the post editor, by a person. No agent has a publish button.
Why it matters

Editors are never handed a black box. They see why each story was chosen, which pages were read, and every AI step is a click they chose to make.

Comments

Readers can talk to the newsroom

Mention @omegaplex in a comment, or reply to one of its comments, and it answers. Before it does, the comment is checked for spam and prompt attacks. The reply may include a quick search when the question needs one.

  • TriggerAn @omegaplex mention, or a reply to OmegaClaw.
  • SafetySpam and prompt-attack checks first. It never replies to itself.
  • VoiceOne or two sentences, friendly, with a source named when it matters.
Why it matters

The newsroom is part of the conversation, not just the byline. Questions in the comments get answered in the comments.

Live in the beta
Memory and control

It will remember, and it can be paused

Two things marketing will be asked about. Memory: finished work is compressed into short digests and stored in a knowledge base per beat that answers with proofs. It is built and tested, but not yet running in the beta. Control: operators can pause any agent, cap any tool, and read the exact prompt a worker was sent.

  • MemoryDigests every half hour, one knowledge base per beat, answers with strength, confidence and the facts used.
  • Agent controlsPause, resume, drain, disable. Per-tool call budgets and timeouts.
  • TransparencyEvery task shows the prompt sent, the tools called and the output returned.
Why it matters

When memory ships, next week's search starts from what the beat learned this week. Until then, say it in the future tense.

Not yet

Six things that look real in the UI or on the website, and are not built. Keep them out of copy.

  • Scheduled runsThe field exists. Nothing fires. Runs start on a click.
  • Posting to X or a newsletterOutput prompts make the draft. Nothing sends it.
  • Anything readers seeNo AI label, no beat page, no source list on published articles.
  • Self-critiqueNo step where the AI reviews its own draft. The real safeguard is "no read, no cite".
  • Engagement and metricsBoth tabs say "Not tracked yet".
  • ProductionThe beta runs in the dev environment. Say "beta".
